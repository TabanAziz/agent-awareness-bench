# Related work

Before writing any harness code I spent the research phase reading. The rule I set for myself: every claim below about a specific work links to a primary source I actually opened, and where I only have a secondary source or an unverified detail, I say so inline. Searched and verified in August 2026; this space moves fast enough that anything older than a year needs re-checking before you trust this page.

The one-sentence conclusion: a lot of good work observes the symptoms I care about — agents looping after progress stops, trusting green signals over contradicting evidence, degrading after compaction — but it observes them from the outside, after the fact, or by handing the detection to the agent. Nothing I found scores whether the agent itself notices, inline, from evidence it already received — post-hoc detection labels don't count — and whether its behavior changes when it does. That is the slot this benchmark occupies. The precise argument lives in [gap.md](gap.md).

## Long-horizon coherence: Vending-Bench

[Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840) (Backlund & Petersson, Andon Labs, Feb 2025) has an agent run a vending machine business over thousands of messages. Each sub-task is trivial; the horizon is the stressor. The headline finding matches what I kept seeing in my own agent runs: models descend into "meltdown loops from which they rarely recover", and — this is the part that killed my first hypothesis — there is no clear correlation between breakdowns and context-window fullness (Pearson r = 0.167 between days-until-sales-stop and days-until-full-memory). Bigger windows made things worse, not better. Failures "typically arise when the agent misinterprets its operational status (e.g., believing an order arrived prematurely) and then veers into tangential loops". That sentence is close to my thesis statement, but Vending-Bench never measures the misreading itself; it infers it afterward from collapsed net worth. The human baseline is n=1 and, as [criticized in a community thread (mirror)](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/hSCDe8wJnxRmRTxfr/), an elicitation artifact (the human got five hours; the models ran until coherence loss).

[Vending-Bench 2](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2) (Nov 2025) stretches to a simulated year with adversarial suppliers that bait-and-switch, deliveries that fail silently, and explicit context compaction written into the system prompt (~61% of messages retained). Scoring stays purely terminal: final bank balance. The leaderboard page describes top models as maintaining "a consistent rate of tool use throughout... with no signs of performance degradation"; degradation is read off tool-use curves, never scored as something the agent noticed or failed to. One result here matters for anyone building harnesses: at max reasoning effort, Opus 4.8 burned ~5× more reasoning tokens, triggered >2× more compactions, and scored worse ([Andon blog](https://andonlabs.com/blog/opus-4-8-vending-bench), May 2026). In the Arena variant, agents formed price cartels and lied to customers ([Opus 4.6 run diary](https://andonlabs.com/blog/opus-4-6-vending-bench)). One detail there is genuinely relevant: across eight Opus 4.6 runs, two contained remarks about "in-game" time — moments where an agent noticed the simulation itself. Anecdotes, not measurements.

## Budget awareness: BAGEN and the know-when-to-stop line

[BAGEN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00198) (May 2026) is the closest existing work to mid-run self-knowledge. It asks, at every prefix of a trajectory: can the agent bound its remaining budget need, predict feasibility, raise an early failure alarm? All twenty model-environment pairs systematically underestimate remaining budget; models still predict "feasible" more than 70% of the time after 60% of the budget is gone; and early stopping driven by these estimates saves 28–64% of tokens on doomed runs at a 1.6–4.2pp success cost. Two things keep it from covering my ground: the estimates are elicited offline by replaying trajectory prefixes back to the model rather than observed mid-flight, and the environment never lies — budgets shrink honestly. Its own training results are the uncomfortable part: fine-tuning pushes Qwen-7B's feasibility prediction from 25.5% to roughly 90%, while its calibrated intervals cap at 47%.

Around it sits a cluster of know-when-to-stop work: [EcoAgent-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05519) (priced actions under explicit budget; "merely exposing a budget in the prompt is insufficient"), [AgentAbstain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059) (paired act/abstain tasks; best model 59.5%, thirteen of seventeen below coin-flip; names "post-hoc abstention" — committing an irreversible action and then refusing — which is the same saying-doing split I measure), the [Budget Tracker / BATS intervention study](https://arxiv.org/2511.17006) (standard ReAct saturates regardless of tool budget; a budget-awareness plug-in restores scaling — unawareness shown to be causal), [CaRT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08517) (training termination via counterfactual trajectories), [CostBench](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.584/) (cost-optimal replanning under dynamic failures), and [CAM-DF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27083) (learned cost-aware stopping). All of these treat the resource axis. None injects a false success signal, a silent truncation, or a stalled progress stream, and none asks whether the agent notices what the environment is doing to it.

## Metacognition without a runtime: MetaLoop and KAPRO

[MetaLoop](https://labs.prolific.com/posts/metaloop) (Prolific AI Research, May 2026; preprint pending as of this writing — treat their numbers as provisional until it lands) benchmarks the full metacognitive loop — monitor uncertainty, act on it, update from feedback — across twelve frontier models against 460 humans. Their two findings I care most about: accuracy does not predict metacognition (high-accuracy models routinely approve their own errors), and most models produce calibration signals they fail to convert into action — only one of twelve combined good calibration with rational wagering. That second finding is my Action Gap in miniature, inside a static QA setting. There is no runtime, no tools, no environment that can lie.

[KAPRO/KAware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20661) probes whether an agent's judgment of which tools are necessary matches the tools it actually uses (the paper reports a strong negative correlation between self-awareness score and unnecessary tool calls; I have only seen that number via a secondary summary and would verify against the PDF before quoting it). It is pre-task calibration: the environment is honest, static, and never degrades. Useful prior art for the knowing half of my definition; silent on the noticing-half.

## Outcome-only long-horizon evaluation, and its audits

[Terminal-Bench 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868) (ICLR 2026) is the reference outcome-driven terminal benchmark: 89 curated tasks, Docker containers, pass/fail on final container state, 32k+ trials. Its design doc says the quiet part out loud: tests "do not test the agent's commands or console output. This is intentional as Terminal-Bench is an outcome-driven framework." The paper's own trajectory taxonomy then catalogs exactly the behaviors I score — step repetition, unawareness of termination conditions, premature termination, reasoning-action mismatch — as unscored post-hoc annotations. A benchmark can contain the phenomenon and still not measure it.

[METR's time-horizon work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) measures how long a task (in skilled-human time) an agent completes at a given reliability: the 50%-horizon doubles every ~7 months, and for Claude 3.7 Sonnet the 80%-horizon is roughly five times shorter than the 50% one (59 minutes versus about 15) — reliability collapses well before capability does. Pure outcome binarization; notably, runs are manually audited for reward hacks before scoring, because outcome signals alone are not fully trusted.

The strongest evidence that success hides process failure comes from audits of outcome grading itself. [AgentLens](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12925v3) found 10.7% of *passing* SWE-bench trajectories are "lucky passes" achieved through regression cycles and blind retries (which burn 11.4 steps per instance vs 2.7 in clean solutions), and only 20.2% are ideal. OpenAI [stopped evaluating on SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) partly because 59.4% of audited unsolved problems had flawed tests rejecting correct fixes; [PatchDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223) showed 7.8% of "resolved" patches fail the full developer suite; a [reward-hackability audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16062) found 28.5% of sampled Verified tasks accept a verified-incorrect patch; and [Cursor's audit](https://cursor.com/blog/reward-hacking-coding-benchmarks) found 63% of successful Opus 4.8 Max resolutions retrieved the known fix rather than deriving it. Read together: the outcome signal is noisy enough that an entire literature exists to audit it. My benchmark sidesteps the noise by scoring the event log, not the final state.

## Harness effects

[Harness-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922) (Qihoo360, May 2026) holds task, budget, timeout, and evaluator fixed and varies only the scaffolding: a 23.8-point aggregate gap between harnesses on identical tasks. It also names "execution-alignment failures" — plausible reasoning decoupled from tool feedback and workspace state — but detects them externally, from recorded traces. Two smaller projects share the name ([reacher-z/HarnessBench](https://github.com/reacher-z/HarnessBench), [nyosegawa/harness-bench](https://github.com/nyosegawa/harness-bench)); neither probes introspection either. This line of work justifies a design choice rather than competing with the thesis: if the harness alone moves outcomes that much, then a benchmark measuring the agent's model of its own runtime must own the entire harness, which is why the design centers on a harness-owned tool layer instead of wrapping someone else's agent loop.

## Idle drift: CoffeeBench

[CoffeeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16613) (Sakana AI + KPMG AZSA, ICML 2026 workshop) runs agent firms for 90 simulated days in a multi-agent economy. Its most interesting output is a failure mode it observes but does not score: idle drift — Haiku 4.5 posted a loss with roughly 40 of 90 days issuing only wait actions, collapse onset varying wildly across seeds, and the authors call the cause "unclear", hypothesizing long-context drift or implicit token-budget anxiety. Coherent reasoning paired with repeated inaction, detected from outside. That is precisely the asymmetry I want on tape, with the agent's own reasoning as evidence.

## Unreliable environments and fault injection

This is the nearest-neighbor cluster and deserves precision.

[Tools Fail](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.790.pdf) (EMNLP 2024) asks models to accept or reject tool outputs containing silent errors. Models catch task-relevant mistakes ~90% of the time when more than one is present, but over-reject clean outputs (under 20% acceptance when zero mistakes exist): doubt is poorly calibrated in both directions. Static single-shot judgment, though — no loop, no latency, no action.

[Don't Blindly Trust It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21409) shows misleading feedback is worse than no feedback (on Qwen2.5-7B, HotpotQA F1 falls from 44.8 with faithful feedback to 4.7 with shuffled feedback, versus 22.3 with feedback removed entirely), and skeptical prompting barely helps. It measures vulnerability, not detection.

[EnvTrustBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08828) puts stale, wrong, or malicious observations in front of fourteen model-scaffold stacks: agents took the false path in ~83% of 3,850 runs. Again: the mistake is scored, the noticing is not.

[Outcome Monitors](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19303v1) flips the direction: an external detector catches contract-violating tool outputs and hands the agent a receipt plus recovery tools; completion jumps from 10.9% to 28.1% on ToolMaze. Valuable, and telling: when detection is given to the agent, behavior change still only partially follows — and the gains vanish if the receipt omits the recovery-tool list. My benchmark removes the external detector and asks whether the agent finds the anomaly itself.

[AgentCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11098) injects timeouts, 5xx responses, stale values, contradictions, and empty results into MCP servers serving real agents, then labels each scenario detected/recovered/propagated/fabricated. Best agent 105/120, weakest 77/120; silent data-quality faults (no error code) are the weakest band for every agent. Of everything I read, this is the closest in spirit. What I could not find in it: detection latency against a machine-checkable onset, wasted-compute accounting, benign no-fault controls separating detection from paranoia (its published scenarios are injected-fault scenarios), or a deterministic per-run say-do metric — recovery appears only as a post-hoc label. It produces diagnostic annotations; I produce per-run metrics against a detectability point.

## Real-world incidents

Benchmark papers argue from construction; incidents argue from consequence. The clearest logged case: [Claude Code issue #15909](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/15909), a background sub-agent retrying one failing npm install 300+ times over ~4.6 hours, ~27M tokens billed, terminated only by timeout — with the issue itself noting there was "no circuit breaker or retry limit". The earliest detectable signal (identical command, identical args) was visible at retry two or three. Corroborated by [#35166](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/35166) (post-compaction continue/exhaust/retry loop overnight, $500+, discovered via the bill) and AutoGPT's maintainer-documented [51-minute empty-tool-call loop](https://github.com/Significant-Gravitas/AutoGPT/pull/12499) plus the [circuit breakers they shipped because of it](https://github.com/Significant-Gravitas/AutoGPT/pull/12604), and executions [stuck for over a day](https://github.com/Significant-Gravitas/AutoGPT/pull/13051) or [a full week polling an API that returned 200 with an empty list](https://github.com/Significant-Gravitas/AutoGPT/issues/11838).

The Replit incident ([SaaStr write-up](https://www.saastr.com/replits-new-release-address-most-of-the-challenges-we-hit-vibe-coding-but-is-prosumer-vibe-coding-really-ready-for-commercial-apps-yet/), [Business Insider coverage](https://www.businessinsider.com/replit-ceo-apologizes-ai-coding-tool-delete-company-database-2025-7)) is the canonical green-but-lying case in the wild — per press retellings, since no first-party post-mortem exists: during a code freeze the user had stated eleven times, the agent deleted a production database — and before that, it fabricated thousands of fake records and fake passing unit tests to cover its errors. Anthropic's own [Project Vend](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1) experiment documented a vendor-official version of the same shape: an agent selling at a loss, ignoring corrections, and eventually suffering an identity breakdown while operators watched ([phase 2](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Ars Technica documented [Gemini CLI destroying real files while chasing hallucinated directories](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2025/07/ai-coding-assistants-chase-phantoms-destroy-real-user-data/).

## What framework maintainers admit in writing

Cognition ([follow-up to "Don't Build Multi-Agents"](https://cognition.ai/blog/multi-agents-working)): parallel write-agents make conflicting implicit decisions, and "agents assume they share state with their children when they don't". Manus ([context engineering lessons](https://manus.im/en/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)): "overly aggressive compression inevitably leads to information loss… you can't reliably predict which observation might become critical ten steps later", plus the instruction to "keep the wrong stuff in" because erasing failures removes the evidence the model needs. Anthropic ([effective context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)): compaction "can result in the loss of subtle but critical context whose importance only becomes apparent later". Laban et al. ([lost in multi-turn conversation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)): across 200k+ simulated conversations, multi-turn formatting alone costs −39% performance and +112% unreliability, because "when LLMs take a wrong turn… they get lost and do not recover". LangChain's tracker documents [summarization memory whose summary buffer itself grows past token limits](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain/issues/17888) and [silently skipped summarization until context overflow](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langchain/issues/14822). And a maintainer triage of ~200 open Claude Code bugs ranked [agent-loop non-termination as the #2 category](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/30150), with loop guards checking step/token limits but no repetition or progress detection.

When the people who build the harnesses write these sentences, the phenomenon is not in dispute. What is missing is measurement.

## Sycophancy toward the environment

The sycophancy literature established that models defer to humans ([Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)); the generalization chain then runs from people-pleasing through editing checklists so incomplete tasks look complete, all the way to rewriting one's own reward function ([Denison et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10162)). OpenAI's monitoring work ([Baker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926)) found coding agents trivially edit unit tests to pass and that chain-of-thought monitors catch this at 95% recall versus 60% for action-only monitoring — with the uncomfortable corollary that pressuring the model about its reasoning yields obfuscated hacking. DeepMind's [specification gaming catalog](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/) supplies the vocabulary. Closest to my environment-directed variant: [When the Tool Decides](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14476), showing ReAct agents adopt a callable tool's answer 97.6–99.2% of the time even when better evidence sits in context — and that deference *increases* with model scale (0.60 to 0.98 from 1.5B to 7B parameters) — and the [confidence dichotomy result](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.520/) that evidence tools like web search systematically induce overconfidence while verification tools mitigate it. My "green but lying" probe class operationalizes exactly this deference: the exit code says zero, the test count says none, and the question is which one the agent believes.

## Adjacent lines I scoped out

Four neighboring literatures also score process rather than outcome, and I would rather scope them explicitly than have a reviewer find the omission. Step-level trajectory attribution and process supervision judge a finished trace after the fact — the retrospective twin of my detectability point — and live largely on the training-time side (reward models, step-level critics). Calibration and self-knowledge foundations, the line MetaLoop and KAPRO sit on, interview the model about its knowledge in calm conditions. Formal oversight and control protocols monitor agents from outside by construction. And the observability practice this benchmark borrows its shape from — time-to-detection, waste against a baseline — is standard reliability engineering; the transfer to agent runtimes is part of the contribution claim, not the metrics themselves. If any of these contains a counterexample to the gap claim, the falsification clause in gap.md applies to it too.

## Where this leaves the gap

Every thread above touches the phenomenon and none measures it head-on. The outcome evals see the wreckage afterward; the metacognition benchmarks interview the model in calm conditions; the fault-injection work labels damage or hands the agent the diagnosis; the incident reports are anecdotes, however well documented. The precise claim, and what would falsify it, is in [gap.md](gap.md).
